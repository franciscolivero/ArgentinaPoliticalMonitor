Where the forecast really lives

It hinges on the runoff transfer, not on poll noise

Win probability is almost flat in polling uncertainty but swings violently with the runoff-transfer assumption. That is the honest message: the forecast is a judgment about how the rest of the field splits in a Milei–Kicillof runoff, more than a reading of today’s polls.

What this is, and what it isn’t. This is a transparent structural model, not a machine-learning classifier trained on outcomes. A logistic regression that predicts a single 2027 winner from one past election (2023) has no outcome variation to learn from — it would overfit and report false precision. So 2023 is used for the one thing it can genuinely tell us: how far polls miss. The probability you see is the output of explicit, editable assumptions; treat the wide band, not the headline number, as the real finding.

Method & sources

  • Calibration — 2023 polls vs results. Late first-round polls missed the actual result by ≈3.3 pts RMSE (the Peronist vote was understated ~7 pts); late runoff polls put Milei at 51.8% two-way against an actual 55.65% (≈3.8 pts). The direction of the miss flipped between rounds, so the model uses the size of the error as uncertainty and assumes no fixed direction.
  • Current standing. Milei and Kicillof first-round vote intention from the monitor (head-to-head average and the Tendencias snapshot); approval and the UTDT ICG set the default runoff transfer.
  • Simulation. Each draw perturbs the first-round shares by the calibrated uncertainty, splits the remaining vote in a runoff by the transfer rate (itself drawn with uncertainty), and records a win if Milei clears the first round (≥45%, or ≥40% with a 10-pt lead) or takes the runoff (>50%). 20,000 draws per update.
  • Reference. Polymarket’s implied odds, normalised to a two-way Milei–Kicillof share, shown for contrast — it is a market price, not part of the model.

Sixteen months out, this is a scenario tool, not a prediction. Its job is to make the assumptions explicit and show how much they matter. Built by Francisco Olivero; see the main monitor for the underlying polling and market data.
